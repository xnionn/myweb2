Dandelion Wine

Ray Bradbury

Douglas starts keeping a record of everything that makes him feel alive: new shoes, the first dandelion harvest, and evenings on the porch. On a second reading, the summer adventures matter less to me than the adults trying to make peace with time. Bring a passage you understood differently when you reread it.

Pride and Prejudice

Jane Austen

Elizabeth is a sharp observer, but she is not always right. Notice how often we accept her first impressions before the novel gives us a reason to question them.

Animal Farm

Keep track of who changes the rules, and who still remembers the original ones.

Little Women

Louisa May Alcott

The March sisters want very different futures. Jo gets much of the attention, but this time we are following Amy: what does she give up, and which decisions are really her own?

The Hobbit

J. R. R. Tolkien

Bilbo misses his kitchen almost as much as he fears the journey. The small comforts make the dangerous parts feel closer to home.

A Room of One's Own

Virginia Woolf

Who has the time and space to write? Woolf follows this question through libraries, dining rooms, and an imagined life for Shakespeare's sister. For our discussion, underline one practical obstacle she describes and consider whether it still exists.

Jane Eyre

Charlotte Brontë

Jane insists on being heard, even when speaking costs her something. We will compare the choices she makes at Gateshead, Lowood, and Thornfield.

Ray Bradbury
